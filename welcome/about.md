# About

### How I Work

I build, break, inspect, automate, and improve systems.

I want to understand what is happening underneath: the rules, constraints, dependencies, hidden mechanisms, failure modes, and assumptions that determine how a system actually behaves.

That way of thinking started with video games. I spent hours exploring mechanics, hidden content, alternate paths, and things that were not obvious from the surface. I used manual books, cheats codes, online forums, and whatever else I could find to understand what was actually happening inside the system.

> Later, I found the same satisfaction in computers.

I learned Linux because I wanted to understand and control the machine more effectively. I learned programming because software gave me a way to turn that understanding into something tangible.

Over time, that led me toward `backend systems`, `infrastructure`, `automation`, `security`, `reliability`, and increasingly complex `distributed systems`.

### What I Work On

Today, my work spans software engineering, system architecture, infrastructure, security, reliability, automation, and the analysis of unfamiliar or existing systems.

I am particularly interested in problems where the obvious solution is not enough—where understanding the system underneath is necessary to make it reliable, secure, or useful.

I also have a particular interest in blockchain infrastructure, especially systems with complex rules, interesting constraints, and significant room for engineering improvement.

### This Site

This GitBook is where I document what I learn and build: technical explorations, engineering principles, experiments, security findings, system designs, and the problems I encounter while trying to understand how software systems actually behave.

If something has interesting rules, hidden complexity, and room to make it work better, I will probably want to understand it.
